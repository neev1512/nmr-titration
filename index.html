How to use this tool

There's no code to run. Type your values and the results update right away.

Typical workflow

  1. Plan (tab 1). Enter your protein concentration and a few Kd values you think are plausible. Adjust your ligand concentrations until the circles are spread along the rising part of the curves, not bunched at the flat top.
  2. Get the pipetting recipe (tab 2). Paste the same ligand concentrations and your sample volume. The table shows how many µL to swap between tubes A and B at each step, and the chips show the order in which to measure the points. Press play on the walkthrough (or click a table row) to see each swap animated.
  3. Run the first swap at the bench. Measure A0, B0, and A1.
  4. Estimate Kd early (tab 3). Enter those concentrations and their CSPs. The curve your black circles sit on gives an approximate Kd. If it's far from what you planned for, go back to tab 1 and re-plan the remaining points.

Entering values

  • Separate multiple values with commas, e.g. 0.5, 5, 50. Scientific notation like 1e7 works.
  • Press Enter to jump to the next box (Shift+Enter goes back). After the last box, the page moves to the results.
  • Concentrations are in µM, volumes in µL, and CSPs in ppm.
  • In tabs 2 and 3, put A0 (lowest ligand) first and B0 (highest) last.
  • If something is wrong, a red message says what to fix and the box is outlined. The results come back as soon as you correct it.
  • The sliders under single-value boxes let you drag a value and watch the results change live.
  • Explore a Kd (tabs 1 and 3) draws a dashed test curve you can drag. On tab 3, drag it until it passes through your black circles, then click Add to list to keep it.
  • Examples (Paper, Tight binder, Weak binder) fill in all three tabs at once. Paper restores the notebook's example inputs.

Reading the plots

  • Move the mouse over a plot to read exact values for every Kd at any concentration. It only clicks onto a titration point when you are right on top of one, and holding Alt (or Shift) turns even that off.
  • Zoom: use the + and − buttons in the top-right corner of the plot. They zoom around wherever the crosshair last was, so point at the part you care about first, then press +. Reset (or a double-click) returns to the full view. Inside the plot the cursor only reads values. Zooming changes the view only, not your inputs or the exported data.
  • Moving around while zoomed: swipe sideways with two fingers on the trackpad (or shift-scroll with a mouse) to slide along the x-axis. You can also click the plot and use ← → (Shift for bigger jumps, Home/End for the two ends). Scrolling up and down still scrolls the page, and nothing on the plot ever zooms by itself.
  • Exact readings: Alt+← → step the crosshair itself in small increments, which is handy near the origin. When the plot is not zoomed, the plain arrows do this too.
  • Hover a Kd in the legend to highlight that curve and fade the others (on a touch screen, tap it).

Saving results

  • Export → PDF report puts all your inputs, both plots, the swapping table and the titration points into one document. In the print window, choose Save as PDF.
  • Export → All files (ZIP) downloads everything at once: both plots as PNG, the data behind every plot and table as CSV (opens in Excel), and a text file listing your inputs.
  • Each tab also has its own buttons: Save plot as PNG (high resolution, white background) and Download data (CSV).
  • Nothing is saved automatically. Your inputs reset when you close or reload the page. The only thing remembered is your light/dark choice (the ☾/☀ button at the top).

Good to know

  • Online version: neev1512.github.io/nmr-titration. Open it in Chrome or Edge and click Install app (in Safari: File → Add to Dock) to get it as an app that also works offline.
  • The standalone HTML file works offline too and needs no installation, but it does not update itself — install the app above if you want updates.
  • Updates: the installed app checks for a new version when you open it and every half hour while it is open. When one is ready, a bar offers Reload. Offline, it keeps working on the copy you already have and updates next time you have internet.
  • The model assumes 1:1 binding (P + L ⇌ PL) in fast exchange.
